Meet the team

Sales, Trade-Ops, and Analyst, sharing one company brain.

Reads the RFQ, pulls your costs, and drafts the quote and PI.

Trade-Ops

Gets freight rates, checks documents, and flags market rules.

How they work together

From the RFQ to a quote you can send.

1

The RFQ arrives

A buyer writes by email or WhatsApp, asking for a price and terms.

2

Sales reads it and pulls your costs

The RFQ is turned into a deal card, and costs come from your price list.

3

Trade-Ops checks freight and the buyer

Rates, validity, market rules, and the buyer check are added to the deal.

4

They draft the quote and PI

A price ladder, quotation, and proforma invoice are ready for you to read.

5

You check the price and terms, then send

A reminder is set for two days before the quote expires.

Questions

Which Incoterms can they quote?

They quote the term the buyer asked for, written with a named place and Incoterms 2020. For container shipments they can also add FCA, CPT, or CIP as an alternative.

Where do the costs come from?

From your price list, cost sheet, and the freight rates you have on file, plus an exchange-rate buffer you set.

What if the freight rate expires before shipment?

Trade-Ops flags it on the costing sheet and the draft, so you can ask for a new rate or add a freight-adjustment line before you send.

Can it make a proforma invoice?

Yes. The team drafts a PI with the same figures as the quote, including packing, payment wording, and bank details from your records.

Does it work for my product?

Yes. The examples are coffee, rice, cashew, and spices, and the same team can quote other products from your costs and freight rates — including seafood, furniture, and garments.

Who sends the quote?

You do. You check the price and terms, then send the email, quote, and PI.
